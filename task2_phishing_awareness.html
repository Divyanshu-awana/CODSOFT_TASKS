🎣 Phishing Awareness Program

Spot it. Stop it. Report it. · CodSoft Cyber Security Internship, Task 2

1. What is phishing?

Phishing is a social-engineering attack where criminals impersonate a trusted party (bank, employer, courier, government) to trick you into giving up credentials, money or access, or into installing malware.

  • Email phishing: mass, generic lures.
  • Spear phishing: tailored to you, using your name, role or colleagues.
  • Whaling: targets executives and finance staff.
  • Smishing / Vishing: via SMS / phone calls.
  • Quishing: malicious QR codes.
  • BEC (business email compromise): fake invoices or "CEO" payment requests.

Why it works

Over 90% of breaches start with a human action. Attackers exploit emotion, not software bugs.

2. Spot a suspicious email

Red flags are highlighted in the sample below.

From: PayPaI Support <support@paypa1-secure.com>
Subject: URGENT: Account suspended in 24 hours
Dear Customer,
We detected unusual activity. Verify now or your account will be closed.

Hover: http://paypal.com.verify-login.xyz/auth

Open the attached invoice.html for details.

Checklist

  • Sender: look-alike domain (paypa1, rn vs m), mismatched reply-to.
  • Urgency or threats: "act now", "final notice".
  • Generic greeting and unexpected requests for passwords, OTPs, gift cards.
  • Links: hover (or long-press) before tapping; the real domain is the part just before the first single /.
  • Attachments: .html, .zip, .exe, macro-enabled Office files.
  • Too good to be true: prizes, refunds, job offers.

Tips

  • Type the address yourself or use a bookmark.
  • Check the registered domain (the text before .com/.in).
  • Beware homograph tricks: раypal.com using Cyrillic "а".
  • Shopping sites: check seller reviews, domain age, and unusual payment methods.

Authority"This is the CEO, wire funds now."

PretextingFake "IT helpdesk" call asking for your password.
BaitingUSB drive labelled "Salaries 2026" left in a lobby.
MFA fatigueFlooding you with approval prompts until you tap "Yes".

Defence: slow down, verify through a second channel (call the known number), and never share OTPs with anyone.

5. Practical security tips

  • Enable MFA, preferably an authenticator app or passkeys/security keys over SMS.
  • Use a password manager and unique passwords.
  • Keep OS, browser and antivirus updated.
  • Do not open unexpected attachments; preview in a sandbox if needed.
  • Report phishing to your IT/security team; in India use cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930 for financial fraud.
  • If you clicked: disconnect, change passwords from a clean device, enable MFA, alert IT, monitor bank activity.

6. Case studies

Google & Facebook invoice scam (2013–2015)

A Lithuanian fraudster sent fake invoices from a spoofed vendor and obtained over $100M. Lesson: verify payment-detail changes by phone; use dual approval for payments.

Twitter hack (2020)

Attackers phoned employees pretending to be IT support (vishing), got access to internal tools and took over high-profile accounts. Lesson: staff training and phishing-resistant MFA.

RSA SecurID breach (2011)

A spear-phishing email with an Excel attachment titled "2011 Recruitment plan" led to a major compromise. Lesson: targeted lures look routine; restrict attachments and patch.

Fake courier / KYC SMS (common in India)

"Your bank KYC expires today, click link" leads to a clone banking page that steals the OTP. Lesson: banks never ask for OTP, PIN or CVV.
